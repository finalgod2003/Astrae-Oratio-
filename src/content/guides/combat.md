Astrae Oratio uses **3D turn-based combat with real-time reactions**. You plan each turn around a pool of action points, try to crack enemy Break gauges, and react to big enemy attacks with a timed parry. Director Lim Jong-gyu summed up the goal at Tokyo Game Show 2026: the fun should come from **predicting and planning**, not from fast reflexes.

## Quick summary

- **Turn-based**, but enemy attacks can be **parried** with precise timing.
- Actions cost **AP**, so you can chain several actions in a single turn.
- Each character has **basic attacks, combat skills (with cooldowns) and an ultimate** (needs a full gauge).
- Hitting **elemental weaknesses** drains an enemy's **Break gauge** fast.
- Only **one character fights on the field at a time**; you **tag** teammates in and out.
- A **Dominion Skill** fires once its conditions are met, resetting the caster's cooldowns and adding buffs.
- Every CBT character had one of six **roles**: Attacker, Breaker, Sweeper, Defender, Supporter or Anchor.

## Turns and real-time reactions {#overview}

Battles are fought in turns, but the game layers real-time elements on top. While the enemy acts, you can watch its animation and respond — most notably with a [parry](#parry). Tokyo Game Show coverage describes the result as a turn-based RPG with real-time response elements rather than an action game.

## AP: chaining actions {#ap}

The battle screen shows **AP** (action points) at the bottom. Every action you take spends AP, which lets you string several actions together in one turn instead of being limited to one move per character. Managing that pool — when to spend it, and what to spend it on — is the core of each turn.

## Basic attacks, skills and ultimates {#skills}

Characters have three kinds of actions:

| Action | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Basic attack | Always available. Using basic attacks **shortens your combat skill cooldowns**. |
| Combat skill | Stronger abilities with **individual cooldowns**. |
| Ultimate | The big finisher. Needs a **full gauge** before you can use it. |

## Break {#break}

Enemies have a **Break gauge**. Attacks that target an enemy's **elemental weakness** deplete it quickly, and some attacks are designed specifically to cut large chunks off it. Once an enemy is broken, you get a window to deal heavy damage.

The practical takeaway from early footage: time your Breaks so they land just before the turns of characters who can exploit the enemy's weakness.

## Parry {#parry}

Powerful incoming attacks can be **parried** by pressing a button with precise timing. There is **no timing bar** to help you — you read the enemy's animation cues and react, which is why the developers stress observation over reflexes.

## Tag and party {#tag}

Only **one character is active on the field** at any time; the rest wait on standby. Swapping who is in front is called **tagging**, and depending on the character or their role, a swap can trigger **tag effects**. Standby characters are not completely safe: certain **area-of-effect attacks** hit them too.

Tokyo Game Show coverage described three-character tag teams. The CBT battle UI showed four portraits at the bottom of the screen, so treat the exact party size as unconfirmed until launch.

## Dominion Skill (Declaration of Dominion) {#dominion-skill}

The official CBT guidebook explains the **Dominion Skill** (領地宣言, "Declaration of Dominion"):

1. Each character has **activation conditions**. Once they're met during battle, the Dominion Skill becomes available.
2. **Tapping** it fires the skill **immediately**.
3. On use, **all of the caster's cooldowns are reset** and **additional buffs** are applied.

Tokyo Game Show coverage adds that a Dominion Skill also **recovers AP**, which makes it a natural turning point in a fight. The name comes from the lore: magicians draw power from their [dominion](/world/#dominion).

## Artifact Liberation {#artifact-liberation}

In a TGS 2026 interview, the developers named **Artifact Liberation** as another way to turn a battle around, alongside the Dominion Skill. No further details have been published. In the lore, [Artifacts](/world/#artifact) are the weapons that give form to a magician's will.

## Combat content and difficulty {#modes}

The CBT offered battle quests in **Minato** and **Shinjuku** at four difficulty levels — **Normal, Hard, Very Hard and Challenge** — plus:

- **[Score Trial](/guides/progression/#score-trial)** — boss fights under varying conditions.
- **[Simulation Room](/guides/progression/#simulation-room)** — a dungeon for growth materials.
- A **raid** mode that was newly revealed for the test.

## Practical tips from the CBT era {#tips}

These are general pointers based on how the systems are described, not a meta analysis:

- **Do the Training mode.** Basic Training covers the fundamentals and Advanced Training walks through specific characters — the fastest way to learn each kit. See [Training](/guides/progression/#training).
- **Plan around Break windows.** Line up your Breakers first, then swap in the characters who hit the enemy's weakness hardest.
- **Spend basic attacks deliberately.** They shorten skill cooldowns, so they are not wasted turns.
- **Hold your Dominion Skill for a moment that matters.** A full cooldown reset is worth the most right after you've used your best skills.
- **Watch, then parry.** There is no timing bar, so learn each boss's tells.
